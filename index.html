The smallest bills can end a semester.

A fee or a repair that most of us would barely notice can stop a Hancock student cold. Each of these happened to a real Hancock student. Gifts from people like you covered them, so the student could keep going.

A fair question

"The Foundation holds $31.3 million. Why does it need my gift?"

Most of that money sits in 127 separate endowed funds. Each was started by a family, a business, or a friend of the college who wanted a promise to outlast them. All but three can do only the one thing their founder asked.

None of them can pay a $166 enrollment fee this week.

A gift you make now, with no strings attached, can. It goes to the President's Circle, which moves the week a need shows up: a fee, a repair, a rent gap. You decide a student you haven't met is worth it. The people closest to that student make sure your gift gets there in time.

The semester no one plans for

Alexa's way back

Alexa Nunez Palos came to Hancock as a Promise student, working toward a degree she hoped would carry her to a four-year university. Last spring, something happened at work that forced her to drop all of her classes to recover.

When she was ready to come back, she no longer qualified for financial aid. Stepping away to heal had cost her the support she started with.

No scholarship is written for that. Unrestricted gifts paid her fall tuition, so she could keep going toward her degree.

“Due to your generosity, I can focus on healing and pursuing my educational goals.”

Alexa Nunez Palos, Hancock student
